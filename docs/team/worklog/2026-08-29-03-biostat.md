### 2026-08-29 | biostat | 2h30m

Built the sun and moon half of the tide engine — the part that will shade the tide chart
with night, twilight and daylight, and show the moon phase.

- **Sunrise, sunset, twilight and solar noon for any place on Earth.** Written from NOAA's
  published solar algorithm rather than installed from a package, because this maths has to
  be rewritten in Swift for a phone app later and a JavaScript package cannot come along.
- **Checked it against the U.S. Naval Observatory rather than against itself.** 514 sun
  events, ten places from Singapore to the Arctic, dates from 1905 to 2050. Worst
  disagreement 34 seconds, and the Observatory only publishes to the nearest minute, so most
  of that is their rounding. Moon brightness agrees to a third of a percentage point.
- **Handled the places where the sun does not rise.** Inside the Arctic Circle in midwinter
  there is no sunrise. The code says so, rather than inventing a time that looks plausible.
  Same for the midnight sun. Both are tested.
- **The chart's background shading is guaranteed to have no holes in it.** The list of
  night/twilight/day bands is contiguous by contract: no gaps, no overlaps, covers exactly
  the range asked for, even across several days, even in the polar cases where the answer is
  one single band lasting three days. Tested a full year, day by day, in two places.
- **Daylight saving.** There is no local clock inside this engine — everything is UTC — so
  the clock change cannot break the astronomy. Tested a US spring-forward, a US fall-back
  and an Australian one anyway, because "it cannot happen" is how it happens.
- **Published the reference data as a file both apps will share.** `astro.json` holds the
  Naval Observatory's numbers with a citation on each one, so when the iPhone version is
  written it has to agree with the same authority, not merely with us.
- **Wrote down what these numbers cannot tell you.** A moon phase is not a fishing forecast.
  The write-up spells out why any "full moon = good fishing" claim from a user's own trip
  log would almost certainly be a tide effect, a season effect, or a "which weekend was I
  free" effect in disguise, and asks the interface never to show the phase name without the
  brightness percentage beside it — because we call the moon "full" for nearly four days.
