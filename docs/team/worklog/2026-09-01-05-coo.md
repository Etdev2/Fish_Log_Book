### 2026-09-01 | coo | one session

Kicked off the Regulations & Fish ID initiative. The founder asked for the whole feature —
architecture through to the catch form — in one session. We got the design work done and
none of the code. Writing down honestly why.

- Ran four specialists in parallel on separate files: architect (system design + a new
  ADR), regulations/data (the real California numbers), fish ID (telling rockfish apart),
  and UX (the screens). All four delivered. Nothing was merged into `main`; this is a
  branch.

- **The thing that went wrong, and it is the important part of this entry.** This machine
  cannot reach the internet except for a short allow-list. Every California Department of
  Fish and Wildlife address, plus NOAA and the federal regulations site, is refused before
  the connection is even made. I checked this myself rather than taking the researcher's
  word for it — even `example.com` is refused, so it is a blanket block, not something
  about those particular sites.

  So we could not read a single official regulation. Web *search* still worked, which
  returns a search engine's summary of a page rather than the page. That is enough to
  learn roughly what a rule says and completely insufficient to tell an angler what they
  may keep. In one case two searches gave opposite answers about whether quillback
  rockfish may be retained at all.

  The researcher did the right thing and refused to fill the gaps from memory, even though
  they had remembered figures for sharks, yellowtail, barracuda and tuna. Those are not in
  the files. A bag limit that is confidently wrong is worse for this product than a blank,
  and it is the one failure that could actually cost somebody a citation. I backed that
  call and I would back it again.

- What exists now is a **work order**, not a dataset: a table of every value we need, our
  best current guess, how good that guess is, which official page to open, and what phrase
  to look for on it. Someone with a working connection can fill it in mechanically.

- Two real bugs in existing code turned up along the way, neither caused by this work. The
  local database upgrade step has an early exit that will silently skip new storage for
  anyone still on the oldest version, and the check that makes sure every rule has test
  data does not look inside subfolders, so three existing rule folders are unchecked. Both
  are written up. Neither is fixed here.

- A blocker for whoever picks this up: the app's fish list has one entry for "Rockfish" and
  none for the twenty individual species. The whole point of the identification feature is
  telling those twenty apart, so that list has to be extended before any of it can work.
  That is the first task, not a detail.
