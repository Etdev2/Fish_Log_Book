### 2026-09-03 | head-dev | 1h

- The founder liked the idea of fish identification as well as whales, so built that too.
  There is now a Fish ID page listing everything the app can help identify.
- Two new fish packs, chosen because getting them wrong actually costs you something:
  1. **Pacific salmon.** In much of Washington, Oregon and Alaska you are legally required
     to know which salmon you have before you keep it, and seasons differ per species on
     the same river on the same day. The official key is two things you can read on the
     fish in your hands: what the spots do on the tail, and the colour of the gum line
     when you pull the lip back. Six fish including steelhead, which people mistake for a
     salmon constantly.
  2. **Southern California bass.** Calico, barred and spotted sand bass share a limit and
     are confused daily. The department's own tell is whether the third spine of the back
     fin is longer than its neighbours. I also put giant sea bass in the pack, because the
     juveniles are orange with black spots and get taken by mistake — they are protected,
     and the screen now shouts about it before it ranks anything.
- Both keys come from the agencies that write the rules, not from memory, and each pack
  says on screen where its key came from.
- I deliberately did not try to cover all 178 species. A pack earns its place when
  misidentifying costs a citation or a protected fish; covering everything badly would be
  worse than covering nine well.
- Checked in a browser: the classic chinook-versus-coho call works — spots on the upper
  tail lobe with white gums comes back "Likely coho". And a small spotted bass correctly
  puts giant sea bass at the top with a release-only warning above the results.
