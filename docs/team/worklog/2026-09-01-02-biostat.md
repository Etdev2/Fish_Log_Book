  write down a format for storing them. I got the format written. I could not get the

- **Every California and federal government website is blocked from this machine.**
  wildlife.ca.gov, the CDFW document library, the federal regulations site, the state
  map-data portal — all of them refuse the connection. I tried each one directly and
  they all came back blocked, so I stopped rather than trying to sneak around it.
- The only thing that worked was web search, which gives you a search engine's *summary*
  of a page rather than the page. That is not good enough for a bag limit. In this very
  session two summaries, both pointing at official documents, gave opposite answers about
  whether you may keep a quillback rockfish — one said zero, one said one. I have no way
  to tell which is current.
- So I wrote down everything search reported, clearly labelled as unconfirmed, along with
  the exact list of documents someone should open once the websites are reachable. Nobody
  should ship a single number from that file as if it were the law.
- The one thing I refused to do: fill the gaps from memory. I do know roughly what several
  of these limits used to be. Today's date is past what I was trained on, and the quillback
  example shows how fast these change. An app that says "we don't know, check with the
  state" sends someone to look it up. An app that confidently states last season's limit
  sends them home with an illegal fish and a fine.
- The storage format is done and I think it is the useful half of today's work. The hard
  part was California's rockfish rule, where about sixty species share one combined daily
  limit with smaller caps inside it. The format stores that shared number once, so changing
  it is one edit instead of sixty. It also keeps three things strictly apart that are easy
  to muddle: "there is no limit", "we don't know the limit", and "you may not keep this
  fish at all". Those have to look different on screen. A zero that looks like a five is
  the bug I most wanted to make impossible.
- Two other things worth knowing. Sizes are stored in millimetres for the computer but the
  original wording ("22 inches total length") is kept and is what the screen shows, because
  the wording is the legally quotable form. And a "20-fathom line" turns out not to be a
  depth at all — it is a fixed line of published map points that only roughly follows that
  depth. An app that reads a boat's depth sounder and compares it to 20 fathoms would give
  a legally wrong answer near that line.
