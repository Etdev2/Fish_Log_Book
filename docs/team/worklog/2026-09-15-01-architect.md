### 2026-09-15 | architect | ~4h

The founder asked for twelve implementation-ready specifications for turning the app into a
fishing intelligence platform — government partnerships, environmental enrichment, a
fisheries map, an AI fish identifier, fish biometrics, a tournament rebuild, and more. The
brief said to inspect the existing application first and reuse what is there. I did that
before writing anything, and the inspection changed most of the answers.

What I read and ran:

- All 62 database migrations (79 tables), 442 source files, the ontology, the tournament
  architecture document, the ten ADRs, the product spec and roadmap, and the design system.
- The actual application. Installed it, started it, and looked at the screens in a browser
  at phone sizes, taking measurements from the page rather than judging by eye.

The most important thing I found is a mistake, and it is ours. There are **two separate
catch records in the database and they do not know about each other.** The personal fish log
has one, and the tournament system built its own three months ago with no link between them.
So an angler in a tournament logs the same fish twice, personal bests miss every tournament
fish, and the catches with the best evidence carry none of the tide, weather or regulation
information the whole expansion is about. The founder's brief specifically says not to build
a second catch-record system. We already had. Fixing it is one new column and it is the
first ticket.

The second thing: **a feature that was designed two months ago has never actually run.** The
database has all the columns for tide, wind, pressure and water temperature at a catch, and
a field recording whether they have been filled in yet. The answer is always "not yet",
because nothing was ever built to fill them in. That is the single biggest gap between what
this product is and what it claims to be, and it needs no new screen.

Third: the tournament screens are measurably confusing, and I have numbers rather than
opinions. Two different navigation menus on one screen that disagree about whether the
journey has three, four or five steps. The button to log a fish during a live tournament
sits 740 pixels down the page — below the fold on every phone. The registration form shows
"Every angler needs a name" in red before the user has typed anything.

I also had to be honest about things the brief assumes that are not true. Fish Legal is not
a California prototype — it already covers about fifty regions, and the real problem is that
nobody is funded to keep fifty sets of rules current. Photos cannot be used for AI fish
identification because the founder killed server photo storage on cost two weeks ago; that
decision, not the technology, is what blocks it. And the $10-per-licence fee in the
government section is the most attractive idea in the brief and the least likely to survive
a licensing law and an incumbent vendor contract.

Output: fourteen documents in `docs/specs/expansion/`. Twelve specifications, an audit of
what already exists, and a final decision report with the next ten tickets in order and a
list of where I disagree with the brief.
