### 2026-09-03 | head-dev | 1h15m

- Started Fin ID. The founder asked whether it would be easy; the honest answer is that
  the engine is easy and the biology is the whole job, so I did the engine and left the
  biology alone.
- What I did: took the scoring maths out of the rockfish identifier and made it a shared
  engine any fish family can use. The rockfish screen now runs on it and behaves exactly
  as before — its nine existing tests pass untouched, which is the proof.
- Two things the written design asked for that the code never had, both safety features
  rather than polish:
  1. It now refuses to name a winner when it cannot honestly pick one — off a single
     answer, or when the top two are within ten points of each other. It still shows the
     field, it just stops implying the top one is the answer. A near-tie presented as an
     answer is how someone keeps the wrong fish.
  2. Every candidate now says why it is there — which of your answers matched and which
     counted against it. A ranking you cannot check against the fish in your hands is
     worse than no ranking.
- Also removed a small trap: the list of which answers contradict each other used to be
  maintained by hand next to the questions. It is now worked out from the questions
  themselves, so adding a question can no longer leave a stale list behind.
- What I deliberately did not do: add any new fish. The rockfish traits took a
  thousand-line researched document to cover twelve species. Doing that for the rest of
  the vocabulary is real biology work, and this project's own rule is that a wrong
  confident answer is worse than no answer.
- Checked it in a browser: answer one question and the screen now says "one answer is not
  enough to separate these" instead of ranking anyway.
- Checks: tripwires clean, no lint errors, 563 tests, production build green.
- Files: src/core/rules/identification/, rockfish-id.ts, rockfish-wizard.tsx, vectors.
- Next: a second trait pack needs a biologist's afternoon per family, not a developer's.
  Surfperch or the groupers would be the sensible first one.
