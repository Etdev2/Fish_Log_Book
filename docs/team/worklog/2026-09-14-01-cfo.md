- The founder asked for a full business-potential and profitability review of the whole
  venture. Written up in `docs/finance/business-potential-evaluation.md` — 22 sections,
  numbers rather than adjectives, every figure either sourced or labelled an assumption.
- The main finding, said plainly: this repository contains **two businesses**, not one.
  The consumer fishing logbook scores 3.9/10 on its own. The tournament platform we built
  alongside it scores 6.4/10. Leading with tournaments and treating the logbook as the
  angler-side hook scores 6.8/10. Doing both in parallel, which is what is happening now,
  scores 5.2/10 — lower than either focused option.
- Why tournaments win the comparison: break-even needs about **35 tournament directors**
  or about **840 consumer subscribers**. Directors are findable, already spend money, and
  pass the fee to entrants. The live market price is $2 per angler per event (WeighBook).
- Why the consumer line struggles: at $49.99/yr with realistic churn, paid advertising
  loses money on every subscriber (lifetime value about $82, paid acquisition cost $90+).
  It has to be won organically, slowly. Also: the sport itself lost 23% of its anglers in
  2024, so we would be swimming against category churn as well as product churn.
- The honest headline: zero users, zero revenue, nothing ever sold, and the client the
  spec says we ship (native iPhone + Watch, D15) has not been started. Product risk is
  mostly retired; commercial risk is completely untouched.
- Numbers, most likely case: first revenue in 2–3 months if we sell tournaments, break-even
  around month 22, three-year revenue about $120k and profit about $70k. High case is
  $700k revenue. Probability of failure 55%. Capital needed $8.6k lean, $62k recommended.
- Recommendation is **validate cheaply** — about $4,000 and 130 founder hours to find out
  whether tournament directors will pay. Seven kill criteria and a five-phase validation
  plan are in the document so the answer is decided in advance, not argued about later.
- Two risks I want on the record: we cannot hold entry fees without a real legal opinion
  first (budgeted $9k), and the blank-trip logging rate — the thing the whole statistical
  product rests on — has never been measured with a single real user.
