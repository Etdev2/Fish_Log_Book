### 2026-09-15 | head-dev | ~5h

The founder reversed both of the decisions that were blocking about a third of the
expansion plan: photos can be stored on the server again, and the personal fish log will
sync to the server. Both are now written down properly as decisions D28 and D29, and the
old rulings they overturn are marked as reversed in the places they lived, rather than
being quietly contradicted.

Then five pieces of actual work.

**One fish, one record.** The tournament system had been keeping its own separate copy of
every catch, with no link to the angler's own fish log. So a fish caught in a tournament
was logged twice, personal bests missed every tournament fish, and the catches with the
best photos and judging carried none of the tide, weather or legal information that hangs
off the normal log. There is a link now, with four rules checked against a real database:
you must log your fish before entering it, you cannot enter somebody else's fish, one fish
enters one tournament once, and a submitted entry cannot later be pointed at a different
fish. Guests with no account can still compete — that is the only reason the link is
allowed to be empty.

**One place a location can be blurred.** Written the arithmetic that turns an exact
position into a coarse grid square, plus the rules for who may see what and the check that
stops a map showing a square that only three people fish. It is all in one module with a
build failure if anyone writes a second copy elsewhere — I planted a violation to be sure
the failure really fires. The client's maths and the database's were checked against each
other on five real coordinates, including southern hemisphere and negative longitude,
which is where the two would disagree first.

**One way through a tournament.** The tournament pages had two menus that disagreed with
each other about whether there were three, four or five steps, and the button to log a
fish during a live tournament sat 740 pixels down the page — below the fold on every
phone. Now there is one menu, no step numbers, and the button sits at 436 pixels, which
clears the fold. I did not hit the tighter 200-pixel target the plan asks for; that needs
a stripped-down live screen which is separate work, and I have said so rather than moving
the goalposts. Also: the "this is demo data, none of it is real" notice used to be the
very last thing on the page, after a whole fake tournament with prize money in it. It is
first now.

**A check that stops all of that coming back.** A script drives a real browser at two
phone sizes and fails the build on sideways scrolling, small tap targets, two menus, step
numbers, or the main button falling below the fold. It found three real problems while I
was writing it, including one of its own making. I also had to correct something I wrote
earlier: I had called the two-row bottom bar "recorded and not fixed", and that was unfair
— the code shows it is a deliberate trade-off with the alternatives measured and rejected.

**The groundwork for weather and tide on a catch.** The database has had space for tide,
wind and water temperature since August and nothing has ever filled it in, because the
job that would do it was never built. I have built the foundation: the rules for which
data to ask for, how far away a source can be before its number stops meaning anything,
and how to decide whether a catch is finished, still waiting, or permanently out of luck.
Tide predictions are now stored as what they are — a model, not a measurement — which
sounds pedantic and is the difference between a dataset a scientist will use and one they
will not.

**What I have NOT built, plainly:** the background job itself. The foundation is done and
tested, but nothing is running yet, because where it runs is a decision I should not make
alone — a scheduled server job and a route the server calls are both reasonable and they
have different costs. That is the next thing to settle.
