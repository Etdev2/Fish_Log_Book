### 2026-09-01 | ux-ui | ~2h

Designed the new "Regulations & Fish ID" area — the part of the app that answers
"I caught this fish, can I keep it, and am I legal here?" Wrote the full design
specification and added the colours it needs. No app screens were built; that is the
next person's job.

- **The nav question.** The bottom bar has five slots and they were full. I took
  Settings out of the bar and put Rules in its place, rather than squeezing in a sixth.
  A sixth would force the labels below the readable size floor. Settings is the thing
  people open twice a season at the dock; the rules are the thing people open with a
  fish in their hand. "Rules" is also a shorter word than "Settings", so the smallest
  phones get slightly more room, not less. Settings still exists and moves to the top
  of the Calendar screen.
- **The four answers get four colours, four words, four shapes.** Keep is green,
  Release is red, Restricted is violet, and Unknown is deliberately colourless. Violet
  matters: the obvious choice for "restricted" was amber, but to a colour-blind angler
  in sunglasses amber and red look the same, and confusing "restricted" with "release"
  is the mistake that gets someone a ticket. Every answer also carries a word, a shape,
  and a plain sentence, so it still reads correctly in black and white.
- **Being honest about what we don't know.** The regulation data will ship with some
  values nobody has checked and some we simply don't have. "No limit", "unknown" and
  "you may keep zero" are three completely different facts and I made rules so they can
  never look alike — no dashes, no blank cells, no bare zeros anywhere in this feature.
  If the data is unchecked or more than a month old, a green "keep" answer is
  automatically downgraded to a cautious one. When we aren't sure, the screen tells the
  angler to put the fish back. We never imply the law says something we haven't
  confirmed.
- **The zero-tap path.** The rules card also appears automatically inside the existing
  Log a Catch flow the moment a species is picked, so in the most common case nobody
  navigates anywhere. It never blocks saving a catch — a fish that was caught was
  caught, and an app that argues with you is an app you stop using.
