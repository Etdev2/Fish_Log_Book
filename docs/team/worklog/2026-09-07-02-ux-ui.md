### 2026-09-07 | ux-ui + architect | 4h

Tournament redesign, part two: the registration form and paying for it. Founder brief —
register your anglers and captain, choose several events and jackpots at once, and pay
there. Plus: add a crypto option via MetaMask or any other wallet, on top of Stripe.

- **Most of this was already built and unreachable.** The payment port, a Stripe provider,
  a crypto provider, and the whole order/payment/refund/prize-pool schema were all in the
  repository with nothing on screen. Wrote ADR 010 to record what the architecture already
  implied and to settle what it did not.
- **One order, many items.** Ticking three events and two jackpots makes one order with
  five lines and one total, paid once. The alternative — an order per event — means paying
  three times and being half-registered when the second card fails.
- **Crypto is a second provider, not a second checkout.** Same port as Stripe, same screen,
  same bill. Prices are always quoted in dollars and the wallet sends a dollar-pegged
  token, so a tournament never becomes a bet on the exchange rate. The order is only paid
  when the transaction is confirmed on-chain — a hash is a promise, not a receipt — so the
  screen has a real "waiting for the network" state that somebody actually sees.
- **Nothing can charge a real card or send a real transaction.** No live key exists here.
  The test gateways drive the *real* provider logic, including the chain, asset, recipient
  and amount checks, so what is being exercised is the payment code rather than a mock of
  it. The banner on the screen says so.
- **Wrote the ERC-20 encoding by hand rather than adding a web3 library** — one function
  selector and two 32-byte words, forty lines, no supply chain, and pinned against known
  values in tests. A wrong byte there sends somebody's entry fee to the wrong address with
  no undo, so it is the most heavily tested file in the change.
- **The four questions the founder handed to the architect and UX are ruled in ADR 010 §4**
  and built: the captain's phone is required (this is the 4am cancellation call), each
  jackpot shows its own pot and count at the point of choice, crew stays editable while the
  money does not, and the refund policy sits above the pay button — one per event, because
  one order can span two hosts on different terms.
- **Created the two tables that were referenced and never existed** — `tournament_division`
  (which is what a jackpot is) and `tournament_award_category`. The repo's own RLS coverage
  test caught that I had not locked them down, which is exactly what it is for.

Checked: `npm run verify` clean (8 lint warnings, all pre-existing elsewhere), `npm run
build` clean, 931 tests. Walked the whole thing in a browser at 390px against the
production build: filled a crew, ticked two events and two jackpots, saw both refund
policies, paid $900 by card, and separately took a crypto quote, sent it, watched it
confirm, and saw a declined card fail cleanly. No console errors.
