### 2026-09-03 | head-dev | 1h30m

- I had misread what the founder meant by Fin ID. They meant whales and dolphins — a
  dorsal fin seen once, at distance, from a moving boat — not fish. Built that.
- Good news I did not expect: marine mammals are a much easier subject than fish. There
  are thirteen animals worth knowing on both coasts rather than a hundred and seventy-eight,
  their marks are genuinely distinctive, and NOAA publishes the identification guidance for
  free. So the thing I said would need a biologist's afternoon per family turned out to be
  a researched afternoon total for the whole pack.
- Thirteen animals: gray, humpback, blue, fin, minke, North Atlantic right and killer
  whales; bottlenose, common, Pacific white-sided and Risso's dolphins; Dall's and harbour
  porpoises. Traits taken from NOAA species pages and their dolphin-versus-porpoise guide,
  not from memory, and the pack says so.
- Seven questions, all answerable from a hundred yards away, because that is where the law
  requires the boat to stay. Blow shape, the fin, size against your own boat, whether it
  lifted its tail, markings, how many, and what it was doing.
- The safety posture is the part I want on the record. This screen never helps anyone
  decide to keep anything — every animal on it is protected. NOAA's distances sit at the
  top of the page before any answer is given, not as a footnote after the app has told
  someone what they are looking at. There is a stranding hotline at the bottom and an
  instruction not to get in the water.
- It runs on the identification engine I extracted earlier today, unchanged. Adding whales
  was a data file. That was the whole point of doing the extraction first.
- Tested the pairs people actually confuse: gray versus humpback, blue versus fin, common
  versus bottlenose, Dall's porpoise versus a small orca. And one case where it should
  refuse to answer — a small dark triangle of a fin is genuinely ambiguous, and it says so
  rather than guessing.
- Checked in a browser: answering the three gray whale marks gives "Likely gray whale" at
  77%.
