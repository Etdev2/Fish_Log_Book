### 2026-09-01 | architect | 90m

- Designed the "Regulations & Fish ID" area: the part of the app that answers "I am here
  today, I caught this fish, can I keep it and am I legal". It has to work with no phone
  signal, forty minutes offshore.
- The main decision: fishing rules are **data we send to the phone**, not code inside the
  app. When a bag limit changes we publish a new dataset; nobody has to release a new
  version of the app, and an emergency closure can reach an angler the same day.
- Every rule carries where it came from — which agency, which document, the link, when the
  agency last changed it, when a human last checked it. If the data is old the app says so
  in plain words and stops offering a "keep this fish" button.
- Boundaries (marine protected areas, closures, depth lines) are stored as map shapes and
  the app works out which one you are standing in, on the phone, offline. If your GPS is
  not accurate enough to tell which side of a line you are on, the app assumes the stricter
  side. That will occasionally annoy someone standing just outside a protected area. It is
  the right way round to be wrong.
- Fish identification gives you a **ranked shortlist with a confidence band**, never a
  percentage and never a single confident answer. A future photo-recognition feature can
  feed into it, but it can never on its own decide what a fish is, and it can never reach
  the legal part of the app at all. Only a person tapping "yes, that is what it is" can.
- When you log a catch, the app saves a frozen copy of what the rules said at that moment.
  If the rules change next month, your September catch still shows what you were told in
  September.
- **The map.** The founder approved adding Leaflet and wants a real map. My answer: yes to
  the map, no to downloading satellite/street imagery for offline use. Useful imagery for
  this coast is 150-400 MB and an iPhone will quietly delete it — possibly taking the rules
  with it. Instead the map draws the actual boundary lines, a simple coastline, and your
  position with its accuracy circle. It looks plainer. It cannot disagree with the answer
  above it, and it works exactly the same with no signal. If people genuinely cannot
  orient on the water without imagery, we revisit it as an optional download.
- **Navigation.** The bottom bar has five slots and they are full. I recommend "Setup"
  moves off the bar (it is a once-per-trip thing you reach from the trip screen) and
  "Rules" takes its place. Separately, and not optional: the catch form itself gets a "Can
  I keep it?" link, which is where people will actually ask the question.
- Late in the session another agent's research on the actual California rules landed. Two
  things in it changed my design, so I folded them in: the twenty named rockfishes the ID
  wizard needs **do not exist in this app's species list yet**, and the first real dataset
  is blocked until someone adds them; and California's own map of the protected areas
  comes with a warning from the state saying it is not a legal boundary, so our map has to
  repeat that warning and be extra cautious near those lines.
